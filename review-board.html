Escalated to the designer

The Disagreement

Conflicts from Round 2 that the board could not settle.

A. What should a full-squad wipe cost?

Systems Designer

At least as bad as losing one operator: Downed operators captured or lost on a wipe, or the operation closes or escalates. Fix the cost before adding a confirmation prompt.

Player Psychologist

More than a successful extraction, less than four permanent deaths. Total loss is a death spiral. Capture plus a time-limited rescue, or forfeit the operation and its rewards.

Narrative Critic

Capture, yes. "Closes", no: Sever the Ring, Break the Bastion and Their Last Order are single-path story missions.

Adversarial QA

Any penalty without a roster floor becomes a softlock. In Breakout the squad is one prisoner, so one failure would end the campaign.

EscalatedChoose the wipe outcome together with a roster floor, a save model, and how story missions behave after failure. FL: the cost depends on the undefined save model.

B. Side missions: generator, curated pool, or not in the slice?

Feasibility Lead

A curated, pre-verified pool fixes verification, economy and farming together. Week-8 target: Act 1 plus one side-mission type.

Business Analyst

No side missions in the slice. A capped pool makes the runtime generator overbuilt: "It cannot be both."

Systems Designer

A finite pool only with a pool size calculated from the progression target and a guaranteed-recruit floor.

Adversarial QA

A fixed pool makes the roster softlock real once it runs out; an unlimited generator makes loss trivial. Decide both together.

EscalatedWhether side missions are in the slice, which model, and the pool size.

C. How should the bench get used?

Systems Designer

A carry-over cost such as recovery time. Shared bench XP would make rotation cosmetic.

Player Psychologist

Rewards, not penalties. With no campaign clock, injury timers add friction and double-punish anxious players.

Feasibility Lead

Declare rotation optional; the bench exists for loss recovery. The only option that fits the schedule.

EscalatedIs rotation a designed pressure, and if so, penalty-driven or reward-driven?

D. Fix narrative gaps by adding content, or by cutting systems?

Narrative Critic

A creator cut needs cheap narrative replacements: generated backgrounds, loss-reactive lines, a named handler and Directorate commander. Branch previews should show the people affected first.

Business Analyst · Feasibility Lead

Each addition stacks scope on a BLOCKING plan. Subtract instead: stop the kit lockouts; use text plus one voiced handler, with the antagonist appearing through intercepted orders.

EscalatedThe minimum authored narrative needed for the loss and rescue pillars within scope. PP: moral framing only after lockouts are softened.

E. Severity of market positioning

Business Analyst

Major The wipe exploit sinks the only hook against XCOM 2; the insider-resistance premise is an unused differentiator.

Feasibility Lead

Minor Until the document says commercial release is a goal. Positioning doesn't change what can be built in eight weeks.

EscalatedIs commercial release a goal, or is this coursework only?

F. Severity of the undefined opening prisoner

Narrative Critic

Major It decides who the protagonist is and must be settled before any voiceover script.

Player Psychologist

Minor A dependency: an avatar either can't die, undercutting loss, or can, breaking the framing. AQA adds that it's a missing game-over condition.

EscalatedIs the player an operator, the commander, or the prisoner, and can that character be permanently lost?

Cheap fixes

Quick Wins

  1. Make line 308 require the same explicit leave-behind commitment for Downed operators as for standing ones, matching line 117. AQA · PP R1 · NC X5
  2. Make mission objectives indestructible engine-wide: "a one-line rule" that removes a class of dead-end states. FL D · AQA #5, A
  3. Let choice pairs vary rewards or timing but never lock a kit; at minimum, keep any branch-opened kit buyable. FL F · BA C4 · SD K4

Drill-down

Full Board

Round 1 findings with Round 1 severity; Round 2 changes noted beneath each. Cross-examination follows.

Systems Designer

Round 1 findings

Blocking

1. Failing the mission costs less than losing one operator.

Permanent loss happens only on a successful extraction (L308). A full wipe fails with no stated cost, and the same map can be replayed (L312, L326).

Round 2: stays Blocking, scope widened: friendly fire, save policy, and "secure the location" missions that have no extraction step.

Major

2. The economy has no numbers, no stated sinks and no limit on farming.

No XP, credit, training-step or price values. It's never said whether side missions refill, so grinding could flatten the difficulty curve. Credit sinks run out once the squad is equipped.

Round 2: stays Major; absorbs QA's Field Orders loop; any finite pool needs a pool-size calculation.

Major

3. Nothing carries over between missions, so the bench has no systemic job.

Health and gear reset each mission. The strongest strategy is one trained A-team plus a fresh recruit when a specialist kit is needed.

Round 2: stays Major, reframed as "loss and rotation have almost no mechanical cost".

Major

4. Branch choices lock whole kits, but the difficulty curve assumes all kits exist.

Medic/Captain locks one kit; Long Reach/Heavy Hands appears to lock Sniper or Minigun. With 16 possible paths the Balance Agent has no defined target.

Round 2: stays Major; adds the lose-it-twice death spiral; branch-opened kits must stay buyable.

Minor

5. Pressure only starts after the objective; reinforcement rules are undefined.

Slow, overwatch-heavy play is the low-risk default during the approach. "Sustained" reinforcements have no rate, cap or end condition.

Round 2: upgraded to Major. Credits-per-kill concern withdrawn (L404); only the XP source is still open.

Round 2 — Cross-examination

  • vs PsychologistA confirmation prompt shipped before failure has a cost points players to the free reset. Fix failure cost first; failure cost and save policy must be designed together.
  • vs PsychologistBench XP would remove the last difference between deployed and reserve operators; recruits already catch up to the story baseline.
  • vs Adversarial QAAgrees with the Field Orders gap, not its severity: MINOR, folded into "no numbers". The Suppressing Fire board lock is MAJOR.
  • vs FL / BAA finite curated pool is better for the systems, but only with a pool-size calculation and a guaranteed-recruit floor.
  • ConnectionFriendly fire makes the wipe a one-click reset; the Downed damage rule and the failure-cost rule are one decision.
  • ConnectionReplacements arrive at the story baseline, so deaths get cheaper as the campaign goes on, and nothing in the story reacts to them.
  • ConnectionExtraction is the only pressure phase, has contradictory end rules, and can be shut off by uncapped Suppressing Fire.
  • ConnectionA branch-locked kit can be lost twice: the only Medic kit can go with its carrier.
  • ConnectionA guaranteed minimum toolset cuts tuning from 16 combinations to 2.

Narrative Critic

Round 1 findings

Major

1. The Ring, the insider resistance and the "hint of fantasy" are set up and dropped.

The Ring is severed in Act 1 and never mentioned again. The resistance's origin as the Directorate's own logistics staff is never used. Rated close to BLOCKING.

Round 2: holds Major, priority raised: the unused insider premise is the missing differentiator.

Major

2. The player's identity contradicts itself.

One of the operators (L422), the commander of the squad (L29), or an unexplained unarmed prisoner (L214, L511).

Round 2: held at Major against PP's MINOR.

Major

3. No authored characters on either side; the antagonist has no face or motive.

No character is named, so Their Last Order is fought against an enemy the player never met. The voiceover contract needs speaker identities the design doesn't supply.

Round 2: holds Major; minimum is a named handler, a named Directorate commander, and one line each for rescued characters.

Major

4. "Bring your operators home" is undercut by how loss and failure are framed.

The authored story never acknowledges loss, and the failure rules make a total wipe cheaper than losing one operator.

Round 2: narrowed to the narrative side; notes L380 makes the inversion an ambiguity.

Minor

5. Exclusive choices are framed as loadout picks, hiding moral stakes.

Leaving civilians imprisoned to take a weapons factory is presented as an equipment pick, and the road not taken disappears.

Round 2: upgraded to Major.

Round 2 — Cross-examination

  • vs BA / FLCutting the creator is only safe if paired with cheap narrative carriers: generated backgrounds, loss-reactive lines, richer memorial text.
  • vs SystemsBacks "captured on a wipe"; rejects "the operation closes", which would break single-path story missions.
  • vs PsychologistHolds the opening prisoner at MAJOR: it decides who the story is about. Branch previews should lead with the people left behind, then the kit.
  • ConnectionThe branch named after the tagline removes the Medic if the player chooses against it.
  • ConnectionRescued people have no damage rules and no story role, so the rescue pillar has nothing behind it.
  • ConnectionThe unused insider premise is the differentiator BA says is missing against XCOM 2.
  • ConnectionDeciding what "lost" means in the fiction would settle QA's open Downed rules; L117 states the intended rule.

Player Psychologist

Round 1 findings

Blocking

1. Permanent loss is harsher than a full wipe, which pushes players to throw missions.

L308 vs L312/L326. No save or reload rules, and no difficulty or ironman option.

Round 2: stays Blocking; remedy refined so a wipe doesn't become total roster loss.

Major

2. Downed operators are abandoned automatically, without confirmation.

Once the last standing operator reaches the zone, Downed teammates are lost with no prompt and no undo (L308).

Round 2: upgraded to Blocking; the Example of Play (L117) promises the opposite rule.

Major

3. The second mission introduces too much at once; several core rules are never taught.

A Place to Stand adds squad combat, Overwatch and the whole HQ at once. The damage pipeline, reaction fire inside cones, Suppressing Fire and the extraction loss rule are never taught.

Round 2: stays Major, priority raised; friendly fire on Downed allies added as its most severe instance.

Major

4. Kit lockouts are blind and permanent and can remove a core safety tool.

Medic versus Captain is chosen before either is tried; the Medic is the only ranged revive.

Round 2: stays Major, broader scope; would support cutting branching.

Minor

5. The attachment pillar starts late; the first operator's identity is undefined.

The Breakout prisoner isn't said to be customisable or kept on the roster. Bench XP is unstated.

Round 2: split. Bench XP handed to Systems #3; prisoner point flagged as a dependency on Narrative #2.

Round 2 — Cross-examination

  • vs SystemsLosing every Downed operator on a wipe is a death spiral. A wipe should cost more than extraction and less than four deaths: capture plus rescue, or forfeit the operation.
  • vs SystemsUse rewards, not injury timers; with no campaign clock, timers add friction without choice.
  • vs FeasibilityA single body type cuts below the attachment floor; endorses BA's presets-and-callsigns cut, with the prisoner nameable from minute one.
  • vs NarrativeAdding moral weight before softening the lockouts makes players resent the choices more.
  • ConnectionThe Example of Play teaches the wrong extraction rule, likely to become tutorial and store copy.
  • ConnectionUntaught friendly fire could kill a Downed favourite with the player's own ability.
  • ConnectionThe reaction-fire path preview is the only way players learn the cone rule; it must survive any scope cut.
  • ConnectionA scope cut makes onboarding most of what testers see. XCOM players will bring save-scum habits, so the save model isn't neutral.

Feasibility Lead

Round 1 findings

Blocking

1. The document never says what the eight weeks deliver, what exists, or what gets cut.

A full campaign against at most 16 reference cycles, which the document calls iterations, not missions. Jim's own review time is never estimated.

Round 2: unchanged.

Major

2. Generated side missions can't be verified the way the pipeline claims.

Unity assembles them at runtime with no agent involved, so agents can't "check the resulting missions".

Round 2: stays Major; recommendation revised from "cut" to a curated, pre-verified pool.

Major

3. The Level Builder Agent is the critical path; its tool integration and budget are unspecified.

Never says how the agent drives Unity or Blender or how "playable-path checks" run. The 160k-token estimate is optimistic.

Round 2: split. Missing test harness upgraded to Blocking; token budget stays Major.

Major

4. Combat systems are tightly coupled; gameplay AI has no technical design.

Gridless movement, destructible directional cover and mid-path reaction fire interact; the "AI Architecture" section covers only development agents.

Round 2: unchanged; made worse by unbounded control effects.

Minor

5. Customization, action cameras and macOS add unscoped work.

Mixed-vendor gear must fit every body type and survive close camera framing. macOS is a second build target with no checks.

Round 2: customization upgraded to Major; cameras and macOS stay Minor.

Major

New in Round 2. The save and persistence model is unspecified.

Fixing the wipe exploit depends on it, and a mid-mission save would have to serialize destructible gridless state, cones, Downed states and seeds.

Round 2 — Cross-examination

  • vs Systems / QAProposed fixes add systems to an uncut plan. Use the cheapest rule: rotation optional, a fixed reinforcement rule per mission, a numbers table now, late credit sink deferred.
  • vs NarrativeNamed, faced characters cost art and voice. Briefing text plus one voiced handler, antagonist through intercepted orders; the Ring payoff costs almost nothing.
  • vs BusinessMarket positioning is MINOR until the document says commercial release is a goal.
  • ConnectionEvery agent quality gate rests on a test harness nobody specified.
  • ConnectionFixing the wipe exploit exposes an unspecified save system; retries also need seeded AI and damage rolls.
  • ConnectionObjectives should be indestructible engine-wide to avoid unwinnable generated missions.
  • ConnectionChoice pairs should never lock kits; the Warmech is the clearest single cut.

Adversarial QA

Round 1 findings

Blocking

1. Extraction, Downed and failure rules contradict; failing a mission beats succeeding.

L117 requires a revive; L308 ends the mission automatically and loses the Downed operator. A wipe with a retained layout works as a free scouting run.

Round 2: stays Blocking; adds scouting both sides of an exclusive branch.

Major

2. The Downed state has no rules for further damage, targeting or forced movement.

Can a frag or Orbital Strike kill a Downed operator? Does a revive inside a cone trigger Overwatch?

Major

3. Permanent losses can leave the roster unable to field a squad.

No minimum squad size, recruitment timing or rule for lost single-copy equipment. Unlimited side missions are the only recovery path.

Round 2: becomes Blocking if any wipe penalty is adopted.

Major

4. AP-granting and control abilities have no limits.

Field Orders can loop between Captains; Suppressing Fire can freeze every reinforcement approach.

Round 2: narrowed (Captain is branch-gated). Grenade-pin point moved to Minor; board lock stays Major.

Major

5. Objectives and non-combatants have no damage or failure rules.

Consoles, supplies and escorted people can be destroyed or killed with no fail rule.

Round 2: stays Major, widened to generated missions.

Round 2 — Cross-examination

  • vs SystemsA wipe penalty without a roster floor turns a soft exploit into a softlock; in Breakout one failure would end the campaign.
  • vs PsychologistThe cause is the L308 rule, not the input; L507 already separates confirm from End Turn.
  • vs SystemsMission pressure is MAJOR without the kill-XP condition: uncapped reinforcements are a queue the player can freeze.
  • vs FL / BAThe generator decision must also settle the roster floor and ceiling.
  • ConnectionUnchecked generated layouts kept across retries become repeatable dead ends; offers need an abandon or reroll rule.
  • ConnectionFailure returns the player to mission choices, so a deliberate wipe may scout both sides of a branch.
  • ConnectionThe Medic/Captain branch decides which exploits are live; revives are repeatable without limit.
  • ConnectionLosing the player's own operator is a missing game-over condition; Open the Cages depends on escort rules that don't exist.

Business Analyst

Round 1 findings

Blocking

1. No scope boundary between "the game" and "the eight-week deliverable".

About 16 cycles cannot build or finish about 22 authored missions plus everything else. No MVP, priority list or inventory.

Round 2: kept and widened with FL's point on Jim's review time.

Major

2. The character creator works against the document's own top production risk.

Every outfit, kit piece and helmet must fit every body type, for choices the document calls independent of combat.

Round 2: held at Major against FL's MINOR.

Major

3. Branching and generated side missions are costly, and their testing is unresolved.

Each player sees half of 8 branch missions; generated missions can't be checked if assembled at runtime.

Round 2: reframed as a 16-path balance matrix; defers generator detail to FL.

Major

4. No market positioning, competitors or commercial frame.

The "valid attacks hit" hook isn't compared with Gears Tactics, Mario + Rabbids or Into the Breach; the structure follows XCOM 2.

Round 2: a differentiator exists but is unused; fix the failure rule.

Minor

5. Two documents for two audiences.

Sections 1–6 are a game design; 7–8 are an AI-agent pipeline proposal, loosely linked. No harness for the Balance and Testing agents.

Round 2: split. Framing stays Minor; missing harness upgraded to Major.

Round 2 — Cross-examination

  • vs FeasibilityCustomization is MAJOR, not MINOR: it multiplies the stated top constraint.
  • vs FeasibilityNo side-mission type in the week-8 slice; Act 1 plus the HQ loop through menus.
  • vs Systems / QACapping the side-mission pool makes a runtime generator overbuilt.
  • vs Narrative / PPEach proposed addition increases scope; the cheapest fix is subtraction.
  • ConnectionThe wipe exploit sinks the only hook against XCOM 2; the insider resistance is the unused positioning.
  • ConnectionEvery reviewer's rule gaps land on a test harness that doesn't exist.
  • ConnectionOpening HQ functions in stages fixes onboarding and scope together.
