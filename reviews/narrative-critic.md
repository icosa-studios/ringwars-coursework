# Narrative Critic — Round 1

## 1. The finale's emotional payoff depends on characters the document never builds — BLOCKING

**Problem.** *Their Last Order* is where the story is meant to land. The resistance leader dies no matter what. At least one other principal character dies. The player chooses "a course of action that dooms one of those other characters." A sacrifice choice like this only works if the player knows and cares about each character. The document doesn't give the characters enough to support that.

**Passages.**
- Act characters table (Setting and narrative > Act characters). Each character gets one adjective and one attitude: "A gruff man who dislikes the player," "A businesslike man who likes the player." None of them has a name, a goal, a personal stake in Marendel, a flaw, or an arc. We don't learn why the Act II character dislikes the player, or whether that ever resolves. The Act III relationship is described as having no friction at all ("a positive working relationship"), so it has nowhere to go.
- The resistance leader first appears in Act IV ("The player meets the leader behind the resistance") and dies in the last mission. Her death is fixed and has no stated cause or meaning. She is the character the player has known for the shortest time, so the one death the document guarantees is also the one with the least investment behind it.
- "Earlier allies remain part of the campaign" is the only line on how the Act I–III characters persist after the squad "outgrows" their locations. Nothing specifies how they stay present. Without that, the Act I handler is mostly absent from Act II onward, and the Act IV convergence ("relationships built through the earlier Acts converge") rests on relationships that weren't sustained.

**Why it matters.** The sacrifice choice is the document's biggest narrative promise. As written, it asks the player to weigh characters they only know as "the one who likes me" and "the one who doesn't."

## 2. The player's identity is undefined, which breaks the relationship model and the loss model — BLOCKING

**Problem.** The document treats "the player" as two incompatible things. In one reading the player is a specific person inside the fiction. In the other the player is an outside commander of interchangeable, killable operators.

**Passages.**
- "The campaign begins as that resistance starts acting openly, and the player becomes one of its skilled operators."
- "The opening escape introduces a single prisoner." The handler is "the person who gets the player out alive."
- The Act characters "dislike" or "like the player," and the player "fights alongside her."
- But: operators are a roster with "editable callsigns and appearances," "Operator identities remain open to the player's interpretation," and any operator can be "lost during extraction" or die in a side-mission wipe.

**Unanswered questions.** Is the *Breakout* prisoner a persistent avatar? Can that operator die permanently? If so, who does the gruff man dislike after that? If not, the core promise "This is war. You will lose good people" quietly exempts the one person the story is about. Authored dialogue that reacts "to the player" can't be written until this is settled. It is a foundational story decision.

## 3. The story choices and the climax hang on content that is on the cut list — MAJOR

**Problem.** The production cut list names Warmech, Medic, and Captain. Those cuts would hollow out specific narrative beats the document presents as central.

**Passages.**
- Mission choices: "Bring Them Home or Chain of Command — Restore a medical team or recover a field commander, opening the corresponding support kit." The Medic and Captain kits are the whole payoff of this choice. If both kits are cut, the player makes a dramatic choice and gets nothing from either branch.
- Act IV: *Keys to the Giants* is the step that "take[s] heavy equipment," and the narrative summary says "Access, **captured machinery**, and the defeat of command resolve the struggle for Marendel." The Warmech is that machinery. Cutting it removes one of the three stated means of the final victory.
- Technical Strategy says "affected missions remain playable with the available equipment." That speaks to playability, not to story. The document never says what replaces these beats in the fiction.

## 4. Mission choices are framed as resource picks with no moral or story consequence — MAJOR

**Problem.** The design pillar "Raids that advance the resistance" promises that mission goals "have consequences beyond the immediate firefight." The choice table reduces those consequences to loadout unlocks.

**Passages.**
- "Open the Cages or Take the Assembly Line — Rescue civilians and skilled workers, or secure local weapons production." Picking production means leaving people in Directorate cages. The document frames this as "The player chooses a useful outcome" and says the choices "influence preparation." It never says whether anyone reacts, whether the unrescued civilians are ever mentioned again, or whether the Act characters hold a view. That is a strong opportunity for the Act II character's dislike, and it goes unused.
- "Completing one advances the campaign and closes the other opportunity." Closed doors are never acknowledged in the story. Story delivery says Act characters carry "reactions to mission outcomes," but nothing ties those reactions to choices made or roads not taken.
- The finale choice is the opposite case. Its consequences are "record[ed]" and deferred to "a possible DLC continuation," so the base game's only major branching decision has no visible payoff inside the game.

## 5. The setting's thematic premises are set up and then dropped — MINOR

**Problem.** Several premises carry real thematic weight, but nothing develops them.

**Passages.**
- "Nova's resistance grows from the Directorate's own logistics organization," and its leader is "Nova's CEO." A corporate, collaborator-born resistance is a pointed premise: who governs a freed Marendel, and is Nova a liberator or a successor? The document only uses it to explain the navy-and-blue palette. The leader's death at the moment of victory could pay this off, but no reason for it is given.
- The Directorate has no stated motive or face. It appears as "uniform and immaculate" troops, a "garrison," and unnamed "occupation command." There is no antagonist with a voice, so "defeating an occupation commander" in the finale has no character on the other side.
- Severing the Ring "isolates the Directorate garrison," but it also cuts Marendel off from the "wider network of inhabited space." The cost of that to civilians is never raised.
- "A human science-fiction setting with a hint of fantasy." The fantasy element never shows up anywhere else in the document.

## Round 2 — Cross-examination

### Conflicts

**C1. Systems Designer #1 and Adversarial QA #2 (wiping beats extracting): I agree with the diagnosis but disagree with one of the proposed fixes.** Both reviewers show that a story-mission wipe ("Trying again returns to before the failed attempt") costs less than a costly extraction ("Any operator still outside the zone, including a Downed operator, is lost"). Systems Designer offers two fixes. One is "Story-mission retries also roll back after costly wins (a checkpoint the player chooses)." From a narrative standpoint that is the worst available fix. It makes every story-mission loss optional, and the pillar "This is war. You will lose good people. What you do next is how you win" then applies only to side missions, which are the content the story doesn't touch. The theme would hold only in the procedurally generated parts of the game and be switched off in the authored parts. I argue for the other fix, "Story wipes carry a real carry-over cost," or something stronger, because the authored campaign is where loss should mean the most. If the designer wants a reload safety net for accessibility (Player Psychologist #1 makes a fair case for one), it should be an explicit difficulty option, not the default. A default reload would make the GDD's tagline false for the game as shipped.

**C2. Business Analyst #2 (cut the "decorative" features): I object to treating the memorial and identity tools as low-value.** The BA puts "the memorial," "operator achievements and ranks," and the character creator on the list of lower-value features that should be cut before kits. In narrative terms they are not decoration. My Finding #1 shows that the authored Act characters are too thin to carry emotional weight. That leaves the document's other emotional system, "Callsigns, appearance, specialization, and the circumstances of survival or loss create personal stories" (Story delivery), carrying most of the "You will lose good people" promise. The memorial is the only place in the document where a loss is acknowledged after it happens. Cutting it means operators die and the game never mentions them again, so loss stops being a theme and becomes just a hole in the roster. My position: keep the memorial and callsigns, which are cheap and do essential narrative work. Accept Feasibility Lead #5's proposal to cap body types and drop the height slider. Those are the expensive items in the creator. The parts the story needs, a name and a record, cost almost nothing.

**C3. Feasibility Lead #1 (merge choice branches onto one map with a different reward): this would lock in my Finding #4 unless it comes with a narrative minimum.** Feasibility proposes, as a mission-level cut, "make the branch choices share one map with a different reward." As a production move I accept it. But if *Open the Cages* and *Take the Assembly Line* become the same map with a different loot table, the only remaining difference between "rescue people" and "take a factory" is the reward screen. My Finding #4 becomes the shipped design. The fix is cheap and compatible with Feasibility's plan. Each branch needs (a) a different briefing, (b) an Act-character reaction line that names the road not taken, and (c) one later callback (a rescued worker at HQ, or the Act II character bringing up the people left in the cages). That costs a few VO lines, not a mission cycle. I would make it a condition for accepting the merge.

**C4. Player Psychologist #4 (player identity) at MAJOR vs. my #2 at BLOCKING.** We found the same gap. My disagreement is about severity. PP frames it as a player-attachment risk. I keep it BLOCKING because it is upstream of production: per Feasibility #1 and BA #1, a Voiceover agent is scheduled in every cycle, and every Act-character line that says "you" needs an answer to who "you" is. The document itself already hesitates on this. The Act II column heading says the gruff man "dislikes the player," but the beat text says "he does not like *them*," meaning the squad. That slip points to the cheapest fix: write the Act characters' dialogue to the *squad*, or to a commander who is never deployed, and make the Breakout prisoner the first roster operator with no special protection. That keeps permadeath honest and fits the opening line, "Command an elite resistance squad." PP's other option, a "protected avatar operator," would directly contradict the pillar, since it exempts the one character who has to stay alive.

### Connections

**X1. The narrative payoff is concentrated in the riskiest, latest, least-specified content (my #1 with Feasibility #4, Feasibility #1, and BA #1).** Feasibility #4 shows that Act IV's allied NPC squads and the scripted deaths in *Their Last Order* need systems that exist nowhere in Game Mechanics, and that "This content comes last in the schedule ... and has no fallback." Feasibility #1 and BA #1 show the mission budget runs out well before the end of the campaign. Taken with my Finding #1, the result is worse than any of these alone. The document defers nearly all its character development to Act IV: the leader is introduced there, the "four parts, each centered on one of the Act characters" happen there, and the convergence happens there. That is also the content most likely to be cut or rushed. If the schedule slips, the cuts will fall on the only place the characters get built, and the sacrifice choice will land with no setup at all. Recommendation: move character work earlier, where it is cheap. Give each Act character a name, a want, and a recurring presence in HQ briefings across *all* Acts, and treat Act IV as payoff rather than introduction. The handler and the gruff man need to be present in the Act III briefings, or "earlier allies remain part of the campaign" won't actually happen.

**X2. Scripted deaths vs. the Downed system create a ludonarrative contradiction, beyond the rules gap (Adversarial QA #4 with my #1).** QA asks whether principal characters can be Downed and revived. Add the story lens: if the doomed character can be Downed, the player can revive them with a medkit or the Medic's Rescue, and the game then kills them by script anyway. The player watches the systems say "you saved them" and the story say "no." The phrase "at least one of the other three also dies" makes this worse. It suggests a principal could also die through the normal Downed and extraction rules, which would leave the DLC hook ("The campaign records ... the character it doomed") recording an outcome the player never chose. Recommendation: principal characters cannot be Downed in *Their Last Order*. Each death is delivered as an authored beat that follows directly from the player's chosen course, and "at least one" becomes "exactly one, chosen by the player," plus the leader. Since the wipe rule allows a retry, a player can re-run the finale to doom a different character. That is acceptable, but it means the choice needs to be framed so that each option is plausible, which brings us back to Finding #1.

**X3. Silent extraction losses empty the "personal stories" claim (Player Psychologist #2 with my #2 and C2).** PP shows that a Downed operator is lost automatically when the last standing operator extracts, with no warning. The story sees a further problem. The document's emergent narrative depends on "the circumstances of survival or loss." An operator lost to an unconfirmed click has no circumstances, only an accident. Abandoning a comrade is the most dramatic decision the extraction system offers, and the current rule turns it into a UI mistake. PP's mandatory warning is also the narrative fix: the player should have to *choose* to leave someone. That choice is a story moment, and a later Act-character reaction could acknowledge it.

**X4. A DLC hook in a coursework deliverable leaves the finale choice without a payoff (BA #4 with my #4).** BA notes the document can't decide whether it is a coursework build or a commercial product with a "possible DLC continuation." For the story this matters concretely. The course deliverable is "a full singleplayer campaign playthrough ... through the final assault and ending." The ending's only branching consequence is recorded for a DLC that is outside the deliverable. Within the scope being graded, the climactic choice pays off nothing. The base game needs at least an epilogue beat in which a surviving character responds to the choice.

### Revisions

- **#1, keep BLOCKING, broaden the scope.** X1 and BA #5 ("the Act characters are described only as archetypes ... Yet voiceover and cinematics for them are scheduled deliverables") show this is a production blocker as well as a quality problem. Unnamed characters cannot be voiced.
- **#2, keep BLOCKING.** Argued in C4. I'm adding a concrete resolution path: dialogue addressed to the squad, and an unprotected Breakout operator.
- **#3, keep MAJOR, narrow my claim.** Systems Designer #4, Player Psychologist #5, Feasibility #3, Adversarial QA #3, and BA #2 all raised the hollow Medic/Captain choice. My distinct contribution is the narrative summary line, "Access, captured machinery, and the defeat of command resolve the struggle." Cutting the Warmech removes one of the three stated means of victory, so the narrative summary has to be rewritten along with the mission. Everything else in this finding is shared ground and the moderator should merge it.
- **#4, keep MAJOR, raise its priority.** Feasibility's branch-merge proposal (C3) makes this finding more likely to become the shipped design. The cost of fixing it is VO lines and briefings, so it is one of the cheapest high-value fixes on the board.
- **#5, keep MINOR, withdraw one bullet.** I withdraw the "hint of fantasy" point. It is a tone detail and doesn't break any story promise, so it doesn't belong next to the others. The faceless antagonist bullet stays. Combined with X2, a finale with no voiced opponent and with ally deaths that don't follow from the systems means the climax has no dramatic counterweight on either side.
- **New, MAJOR: on story missions the pillar "You will lose good people" is mechanically optional (from C1, combining Systems Designer #1 and Adversarial QA #2).** As written, the authored story is the one place where loss can always be undone and the player is rewarded for undoing it. QA's note that quitting mid-mission is undefined widens the gap, because "Each mission is played once" is the rule that would make loss carry weight. Whatever loss model is chosen, it has to keep the theme intact in the authored campaign, not only in the generated side missions.
