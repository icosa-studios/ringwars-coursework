# Player Psychologist — Round 1

## 1. Permanent squad loss can trap the player in a death spiral, and nothing in the document catches them — MAJOR (close to BLOCKING)

**Problem.** If the whole squad is wiped on a side mission, every deployed operator is gone. Each one has a 45% chance of capture and is otherwise dead for good. Rescue is only a "may". Recruits arrive only at "campaign milestones" and only fill "available roster space". So a player who loses their regular four early in Act I may not have enough operators to field a full squad, and the experienced ones are gone for good. The story mission that follows is harder than the side mission they just lost. If they wipe there, they get a Game Over. The document has no difficulty modes, no rule for a minimum roster or emergency recruits, and no catch-up. The pillar promises "You will lose good people. What you do next is how you win." But "what you do next" isn't backed by any system that keeps the player able to keep playing.

**Source.** Mission outcomes table (side mission wipe: "45% chance of capture; otherwise they are permanently lost"). "Later optional rescue missions *may* offer a chance." Recruitment and loss: "Campaign milestones expand the roster... Recruitment fills available roster space." No difficulty, ironman or save-model section exists. The main-story wipe text says "Trying again returns to before the failed attempt", but it never says whether side-mission losses can be reloaded the same way.

**Why it matters.** The first hard loss is the moment players quit tactics games. At that moment the player needs to see a way forward. Specify a roster floor (for example, guaranteed replacement recruits up to 4), difficulty and save options, and whether a reload undoes side-mission losses.

## 2. Downed operators are lost automatically at extraction, with no stated warning or confirmation — MAJOR

**Problem.** "Any operator still outside the zone, including a Downed operator, is lost when the successful extraction concludes." The mission ends when all standing operators are accounted for. So moving the last standing operator into the zone can silently and permanently lose a Downed teammate, possibly one the player spent the whole campaign building up. Leaving a *standing* operator behind needs an explicit commitment, but leaving a *Downed* one does not. That is the more likely case and the more painful one. Losses the player doesn't see coming feel like the game's fault, not their own, which breaks the "understand the mistake behind a failed one" pillar.

**Source.** Extraction and securing a position. Withdrawal example: "The player must account for them before the final standing operator extracts." Readable tactical decisions pillar. The interface section gives no extraction warning, confirmation, or undo for moves that end the mission. Hold-Space is used for End Turn and Space for confirm, so a mistyped commit is also possible.

**Fix direction.** Before the last standing operator enters the zone, show a mandatory warning listing who will be lost, the same way leaving a standing operator behind is handled. State whether any undo exists for moves.

## 3. The onboarding ramp skips straight from "walk and hide" to the full system — MAJOR

**Problem.** Breakout teaches only movement and cover, with an unarmed prisoner. The very next mission, A Place to Stand, has to introduce at once: squad control, action point budgets and split-move costs, attacks, falling hit chance beyond optimal range, directional cover vs. armor, Overwatch, medkits, Revive and Downed, extraction rules, and (because it sets up HQ) the Barracks, Armoury, War Table, training spend and equipment allocation. Several core systems have no teaching moment at all: enemy grenades and their response window, destructible cover, elevation and ladders, the Page Up/Down floor view, waypoints, Suppressing Fire, and camouflage. The design says the game "keeps information visible". It says nothing about a tutorial, tooltips, glossary or contextual hints for explaining *what that information means* the first time a player sees it.

**Source.** Learning through the campaign (Breakout, then A Place to Stand "introduces standard squad combat and Overwatch while establishing the resistance's headquarters"). Headquarters "becomes available after the resistance takes its initial base". The only pacing note for difficulty is "Rifle Overwatch is used sparingly in gentler encounters." Interface section: previews and indicators, but no explanation layer.

**Fix direction.** Split A Place to Stand's load. Bring in the HQ screens one at a time over the Act I missions. Give each untaught system a named first encounter. Define a contextual-help or tooltip approach for first exposure.

## 4. Who "the player" is in the story is undefined, which clashes with permadeath and roster freedom — MAJOR

**Problem.** The fiction speaks to the player as a single person. They are a prisoner who escapes, "one of its skilled operators", disliked by the Act II character, liked by the Act III one, and met by the leader in Act IV. The mechanics treat the player as a commander of a disposable, customizable roster where anyone can be permanently lost. The document never says whether the Breakout prisoner is a persistent operator, whether that operator can be customized, killed or captured, or what the Act characters' reactions are aimed at once that person is gone. Players who invest in "their" character and lose them, or who never realize the prisoner was "them", will feel the story is about someone else. That weakens the relationship beats the campaign depends on.

**Source.** "The player becomes one of its skilled operators" (Marendel and the occupation). The Act characters table ("dislikes the player", "likes the player"). "The opening escape introduces a single prisoner." Recruitment and loss / Mission outcomes (any operator can be permanently lost). Operator identities "remain open to the player's interpretation".

**Fix direction.** Decide between "player = commander, not an operator" and "player = protected avatar operator". Then state how the avatar is treated under Downed, capture and loss.

## 5. Mutually exclusive choices permanently lock out kits without enough information, and two of the options are on the cut list — MINOR

**Problem.** Four story choices close one option for good, including kit access ("opening the corresponding support kit"). The player only sees a one-line description of the outcome ("precision weapons or heavy fire support"). They can't preview the kit they would lose, and they may already have trained operators who need it. That invites decision paralysis and buyer's remorse, especially for players who value "experimenting with squad composition". Also, Bring Them Home vs. Chain of Command unlocks the Medic or the Captain, which are both listed as kits to cut. If they are cut, the choice is hollow or broken. The Medic is also in the flagship example operation, so players are being shown a kit they may never get.

**Source.** Mission choices table. "Training... within unlocked kit lines." Technical Strategy, "Kits to cut if needed: Warmech, Medic, and Captain". Example operation (Medic in squad).

**Fix direction.** Let players preview what each branch unlocks, for example a trial loadout or HQ training-space access. State whether the locked-out option can still be bought later in the Armoury. Plan a replacement reward for that choice if Medic and Captain are cut.

## Round 2 — Cross-examination

### Conflicts

**C1. Systems Designer #1 / Adversarial QA #2 (wipe beats extraction). I agree with the diagnosis but disagree with one of the proposed fixes.** Both colleagues are right that on story missions, a wipe (free rewind) is better than a costly extraction (permanent losses). Systems Designer's option "Story wipes carry a real carry-over cost" fixes the exploit by making the bad outcome worse. From the player's point of view, that is the wrong direction. It closes the loophole by adding a second route into the death spiral in my Finding 1. A player who wipes on a story mission would then come back to a depleted roster *and* a harder mission, which is exactly where people quit. The player-friendly fix is SD's other option, or QA's: let the player restart a story mission *before resolution* after a costly win, so a painful extraction is something they chose to accept. The stakes then come from the player owning the decision, not from the rules punishing them. I support SD's side-mission fix (abandoned Downed operators get the same capture roll as a wipe) without reservation. It makes the most common loss feel less arbitrary.

**C2. Business Analyst #3 (side missions and capture/rescue as cuttable).** BA treats the side-mission and capture/rescue layer as optional breadth that could be cut. My lens says it is the game's only recovery mechanism. The document's answer to "what if I'm under-equipped" is side missions. Its answer to "I lost my best operator" is capture plus a possible rescue. If that layer is cut while permadeath from extraction stays, the game has all of the loss and none of the comeback. The "What you do next is how you win" pillar would then describe something that doesn't exist. If the layer is cut, the roster floor I asked for in Finding 1 stops being a nice-to-have and becomes mandatory, and capture-on-abandonment should move into story missions.

**C3. Business Analyst #2 (cut the character creator, memorial and ranks before kits).** I partly disagree. The memorial, editable callsigns and ranks are cheap, and they are how players get attached to operators. That attachment is what makes "You will lose good people" hurt in the intended way rather than feel like losing a spreadsheet row. I agree with Feasibility #5 that body-type and height fitting is the expensive part and should be cut down hard. The callsign, a few appearance presets, rank display and the memorial should be protected, because they cost little and carry most of the emotional value.

**C4. Feasibility #1 / Business Analyst #1 vs. my Finding 3 fix.** My Round 1 fix ("give each untaught system a named first encounter", "bring in the HQ screens one at a time over the Act I missions") assumes there is mission capacity to spare, and both colleagues show there isn't. I'm revising the fix, not the finding. Teaching has to come from a cheap contextual layer: first-exposure tooltips, a glossary, and staged HQ screen unlocks inside existing missions. It should not come from new tutorial content. The budget squeeze makes this finding *more* urgent, because a tutorial nobody planned will be the first thing dropped.

### Connections

**X1. Adversarial QA #5 + my Finding 2 means players learn to abandon Downed operators, and then lose them silently.** QA notes that a guaranteed-hit Overwatch may trigger on any AP spend, which would include Revive. If so, every rescue attempt under a watched cone takes guaranteed damage. Players will quickly learn that reviving is a trap and that leaving Downed operators is the rational choice. Then the extraction rule removes those operators with no warning (my Finding 2). The combination teaches the opposite of the premise "bring your operators home". At minimum, Revive needs a clear rule about whether it triggers Overwatch, and the trigger preview needs to show it.

**X2. Systems Designer #1 + my Finding 2 is the most likely rage-quit moment in the design.** Put the two findings together and you get this sequence. The player fights through a hard story mission, extracts successfully, and silently loses three Downed operators. They then realize (or read online) that letting the last operator die would have cost them nothing. That isn't "understanding the mistake" (the pillar). It is learning that the game punished them for trying. The extraction warning I asked for should also tell the player plainly that a restart is available, so the choice is fair.

**X3. Systems Designer #5 (no abort rule) + my Finding 1.** With no abort or withdraw-early rule, a player in a losing side mission has no way to cut their losses. Their only options are to fight on or wipe, and a wipe hits every deployed operator with the 45/55 capture-or-death roll. A death spiral becomes much worse when the player can see the losses coming and can't step back. An early-abort option (with a cost, such as losing objective rewards) gives the player control, and players who feel in control of a loss tend to stay.

**X4. Narrative Critic #4 + my Finding 1 + my Finding 5. Open the Cages could be the roster safety net.** The narrative critic points out that "Rescue civilians and skilled workers" has no consequence. That is the natural place for a recruitment payoff: rescued people join the roster. It would give the moral choice a felt consequence, which addresses the narrative critic's concern. It would also add recruits outside milestones, which helps my death spiral. And it would give the player something more concrete than a one-line description when choosing (my Finding 5). One change, three problems eased.

**X5. Feasibility #4 + Adversarial QA #4 + my Finding 3. The finale is an untaught control scheme at the moment with the highest stakes.** *Their Last Order* doubles the friendly unit count to 8. QA and Feasibility show the document doesn't say who controls the NPCs or how Downed and extraction apply to them. From the player's side, the finale introduces a new control model with no ramp, and does it on the one mission where confusion costs the most. Narrative Critic #1 adds a further risk. If the player can see and revive the character the script has marked to die, and then the character dies anyway, players will read it as the game cheating. They won't read it as tragedy. The doomed character's death must happen in a way the player can't fight against through the systems (a cutscene or a moment outside player control). It should not be a Downed state the player can try to reverse.

### Revisions

- **Finding 1: upgraded to BLOCKING.** Systems Designer #2 and Adversarial QA #1 independently reached the same softlock, and QA showed that the story-mission retry cannot recover from it because the save it returns to is already depleted. A campaign that can become unwinnable without telling the player is a blocking player-experience failure.
- **Finding 4: upgraded to BLOCKING, in agreement with Narrative Critic #2.** In Round 1 I judged only how it affects the player experience. Business Analyst #5 notes that VO and cinematics for the Act characters are scheduled deliverables. Every line that reacts "to the player" depends on this decision, so it blocks production as well as the experience.
- **Finding 5: narrowed, stays MINOR.** I'm withdrawing the cut-list half. Four other reviewers covered it more thoroughly (Systems Designer #4, Feasibility #3, QA #3, Business Analyst #2), and it is a production and systems problem more than a psychology one. What remains is the information problem: players make permanent kit choices with no preview and no statement of whether the lost kit can be bought later.
- **Finding 2: stays MAJOR, rescoped.** It should be merged with Systems Designer #1 in synthesis. The warning is a UI fix, but the underlying unfairness comes from the inconsistent loss model, and fixing only one half leaves the rage-quit sequence in X2 in place.
- **Finding 3: stays MAJOR, fix revised** as described in C4 (a contextual help layer instead of new tutorial content).
